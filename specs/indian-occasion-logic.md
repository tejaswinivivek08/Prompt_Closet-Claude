## 1. Occasion Profiles

### 1.1 Tamil Wedding

**Description:** South Indian Hindu wedding with deep-rooted Brahminical and regional traditions. Known for rich silk sarees, temple jewelry, and strict guest-tier dress hierarchies.

**Guest Tiers (Dress Expectations by Relationship):**

| Tier | Relationship | saree Expectation | lehenga Expectation | Color Rules |
|------|-------------|-------------------|---------------------|-------------|
| 1 | Bride's immediate family (mother, sisters, maternal aunts) | Kanjeevaram silk only; gold zari border mandatory | Acceptable but less common; if worn, silk with heavy work | Bright reds, maroons, greens only; gold borders |
| 2 | Groom's family | Kanjivarams welcomed; Banarasi acceptable | More common; mirrors bride side | Same jewel tones; no restrictions on gold quantity |
| 3 | Friends and colleagues | Any silk saree acceptable; half-saree permitted for younger guests | Fusion lehengas increasingly common | Pastels, ivories, golds accepted |

**Color Rules:**

- **Mandatory avoidance:** Black (considered inauspicious for weddings), White (funeral color; reserved for widowhood)
- **Encouraged:** Gold, Deep Red (wedding color), Maroon, Bottle Green, Royal Blue, Magenta
- **Jewel tones only:** No pastels except for Tier 3 friends/colleagues

**Saree vs Lehenga Expectations:**

- Saree is the default and most respectful choice
- Lehenga is increasing among younger women but should be heavily embellished (light work is considered underdressed)
- Avoid crop-top blouses; traditional full-coverage blouses expected

**Jewelry:**

- Temple jewelry highly encouraged
- Gold is strongly preferred over artificial/tribal jewelry
- Flowers in hair (jasmine, roses) traditional for women

**Regional Nuances:**

- Chennai weddings lean more conservative than Madurai or Coimbatore
- Night weddings (evening 7pm+) have slightly more openness to contemporary cuts

### 1.2 Punjabi Wedding

**Description:** North Indian Sikh/Hindu wedding characterized by vibrant colors, lehenga-forward fashion, and energetic celebrations. Distinctly different from South Indian weddings in almost every dimension.

**Color Rules (Distinct from Tamil):**

- **Openness to:** Pastels (mint, peach, powder blue), Magenta, Fuchsia, Bright Orange, Red, Gold
- **Black avoidance:** Less absolute than Tamil customs; small black accents sometimes appear in bridal trousseau
- **White avoidance:** Less strict than Tamil; white and ivory lehengas have appeared in modern Punjabi fashion
- **Distinctive:** Heavy use of phulkari (embroidery), bright contrasts

**Lehenga Preferred Over Saree:**

- Lehenga is the default; saree is the exception
- Sharara (flared pants with short kurti) increasingly common for guests
- Patiala salwar suits accepted for older relatives

**Dupatta Draping Style:**

- Punjabi brides typically drape dupatta over the left shoulder
- Guests follow similar pattern
- Dupatta often pinned at the right shoulder to allow free movement during bhangra

**Shastra Timings (Auspicious Timing Influence):**

- Weddings traditionally avoid `Rahu Kaal`; morning weddings historically more common in Sikh tradition
- Dress intensity often correlates with ceremony timing: morning pheras = slightly less elaborate than evening

**Regional Nuances:**

- Delhi Punjabi = more formal, heavier embellishment
- Punjabi diaspora (UK, Canada, US) = fusion-heavy; anarkalis accepted

### 1.3 Diwali

**Description:** Festival of Lights celebrating the victory of light over darkness. Dress code is celebratory, auspicious colors are mandatory, and fusion options are widely accepted.

**Colors — Auspicious:**

| Color | Significance | Recommendation |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Gold | Wealth, prosperity, Lakshmi association | Strongly recommended; gold embroidery a plus |
| Red | Luck,婚姻, energy | Auspicious; deep reds preferred over bright neon |
| Green | New beginnings, prosperity, Vishnu association | Safe choice; traditionally for new brides |
| Yellow | Knowledge, happiness, turmeric association | Traditional; especially for puja |
| Orange | Sacred, saffron, renunciation | Acceptable; less popular for fashion |
| Pink | Love, compassion | Soft pinks acceptable; not traditional but modern |

**Colors — Traditionally Avoided:**

- **Black** — associated with Rahu; considered inauspicious
- **Dark Grey** — considered a shade of black; avoid
- **Blue** (certain subcultures) — some communities avoid dark blue as it signifies sorrow

**Fusion Options:**

- Saree with crop-top blouses acceptable for Diwali parties
- Indo-western (dhoti pants + crop top, palazzo + kurta) widely accepted
- Lengha + crop top common among younger women

**Puja Dress Code:**

- Traditional families expect silk or silk-like fabrics for Lakshmi puja
- White avoided; cream, gold, red most appropriate
- Full sleeves preferred; no bare shoulders during puja

**Time-of-Day Considerations:**

- Morning/afternoon puja: Traditional Indian clothing preferred
- Evening parties: Fusion and contemporary options open

### 1.4 Navratri

**Description:** Nine-night festival honoring Goddess Durga. Each day has a designated color rooted in astrology, mythology, and regional tradition. Dress code is both devotional and celebratory.

**Per-Day Color Map:**

| Day | Color | Deity | Significance | Styling Notes |
|-----|-------|-------|-------------|---------------|
| Day 1 | Yellow | Shailputri | First form; welcomed with brightness | Easiest to style; gold accessories natural fit |
| Day 2 | Green | Brahmacharini | Penance, devotion | Emerald green, jade; traditional jewelry |
| Day 3 | Grey | Chandraghanta | bravery, grace | Unusual color; silver accessories help |
| Day 4 | Orange | Kushmanda | Cosmic egg; hidden wealth | Auspicious; easiest color for all ages |
| Day 5 | White | Skandamata | Mother of Kartikeya | Peaceful; pair with heavy jewelry |
| Day 6 | Red | Katyayani | Warrior goddess; sought for marriage | Most popular day color; bridal-inspired styling |
| Day 7 | Blue | Kalaratri | Dark/terrifying form | Royal; gold and kundan work well |
| Day 8 | Pink | Mahagauri | Purity, forgiveness | Softest palette day; pastel lehengas |
| Day 9 | Purple | Siddhidatri | Fulfillment, perfection | Most formal; heaviest embellishment expected |

**Why Each Color:**

- Rooted in Durga's astrological associations and the chakra system
- Days 1-3 = gradation from warm to cool (preparation)
- Days 4-6 = peak intensity (celebration)
- Days 7-9 = deepening devotion (culmination)

**Golu Doll Display Context (Tamil Tradition):**

- Golu stepped display of dolls representing traditions, deities, communities
- Dress reflects the kalash (pot) placed on top — usually new clothes for the goddess
- Guests attending Golu exhibitions should dress in festive tradition; Day 6 red especially auspicious

**Fusion vs Traditional:**

- Traditional: Ghagra cholis, silk sarees, half-sarees
- Fusion accepted: Palazzo + crop top, sharara sets, lehenga with contemporary blouse
- **Avoid:** Black (all 9 days); white (days 4-9 especially)

### 1.5 Temple

**Description:** Hindu temple dress code governed by scriptures (shastras) and regional temple customs. Rules are generally stricter than other occasions and vary significantly between South and North India.

**Universal Rules:**

- **No shorts, capris, or exposed legs** — women must cover below the knee
- **No sleeveless, tank tops, or short sleeves** — shoulders must be covered
- **No low necklines** — blouses or cholis with high necklines mandatory
- **Hair covered** for some temples (especially South Indian)

**Color Rules:**

- **White** — strongly avoided; associated with mourning/widowhood in South Indian temples
- **Black** — avoided; inauspicious color
- **Preferred:** Saffron, yellow, orange, red, cream, gold
- **Odd colors** (bright green, electric blue) — discouraged at traditional temples

**South vs North Temple Differences:**

| Aspect | South Indian Temple | North Indian Temple |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Coverage | Stricter; full sleeves often expected | Elbows may be exposed for some temples |
| Head cover | More common; many temples require | Less common; usually only for inner sanctum |
| Footwear | Removed before entering; socks allowed | Same; sandals vs shoes varies |
| Saree preferred | Yes; pant saris for comfort | Lehenga/churidar more common |
| Color intensity | Muted tones preferred | Brighter colors accepted |

**Regional Nuances:**

- Tirupati: Very strict; only cotton sarees or half-sarees for women
- Vaishno Devi: Pilgrims wear head coverings; chadar tradition
- Siddhivinayak (Mumbai): Less strict; contemporary clothing acceptable outside sanctum

### 1.6 Eid

**Description:** Islamic celebration marking the end of Ramadan. Modesty is the overriding principle. Dress expectations vary significantly by regional culture within India.

**Modest Dress Expectations:**

- **Coverage:** Arms covered to wrist; legs covered to ankles
- **Loose fit:** Clothing should not be form-fitting
- **Non-transparent fabrics:** Thickness and opacity mandatory
- **No extreme cleavage or midriff exposure**

**Preferred Colors:**

- **White and cream** — Eid traditional; signifies purity and new beginnings
- **Pastels** — soft pinks, mint, peach, lavender
- **Emerald green** — deeply traditional; worn by many
- **Gold accents** — jewelry, embroidery on white/cream base
- **Avoid:** Black (not forbidden but considered somber for celebration); overly bright neons

**Fusion Traditional Options:**

- Saree with full-sleeve blouse: Very common among Indian Muslim women
- Anarkali suits: Widely accepted; choose modest lengths
- Palazzo kurta with dupatta: Modern and modest
- Churidar kameez: Traditional and comfortable

**Regional Differences:**

| Region | Dress Style | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Bengali Eid | White saree with red border; white kameez | Sharad mahal tradition; white pure |
| Hyderabadi | Charca (head-to-toe white), heavy gold jewelry | Distinctive; white on white with gold |
| Tamil Muslim | Bizarre (traditional saree), often in pastel | South Indian Muslim tradition |
| Kerala Mappila | Settu mundu (white mundhu with gold border) | Unique to Malabar region |
| North Indian | Anarkali, palazzo suits, saree | More varied; fusion-heavy |

## 2. Cultural Scoring Model

**Purpose:** Rank wardrobe items for a given occasion using a weighted multi-factor scoring algorithm.

### 2.1 Factor Breakdown

| Factor | Weight | Description |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| Cultural Appropriateness | 30% | Is this item allowed/permitted for the occasion? Binary or tiered gate. |
| CLIP Semantic Similarity | 25% | Visual-semantic match between item image and query intent. |
| Formality Match | 25% | Alignment between item formality tier and occasion formality requirement. |
| Color Appropriateness | 15% | How well item color maps to occasion's auspicious/forbidden color rules. |
| Heritage Bonus | 5% | Regional alignment between item's heritage origin and user's stated heritage. |

### 2.2 Cultural Appropriateness Gate (30%)

**Binary Pass/Fail:**

- Items that violate hard rules (e.g., black at Tamil wedding) receive 0 on this factor
- Items that are permitted but not ideal receive 0.7
- Items that are culturally preferred receive 1.0

**Per-Occasion Hard Rules:**

| Occasion | Hard Fail Items |
|----------|----------------|
| Tamil Wedding | Black saree, white saree, synthetic fabric |
| Punjabi Wedding | White lehenga (if traditional family), black outfit |
| Diwali | Black outfit |
| Navratri | Black outfit (all days) |
| Temple | Shorts, sleeveless top, mini skirt |
| Eid | Transparent fabric, tight-fitting clothing, mini dress |

### 2.3 CLIP Semantic Scoring (25%)

- Run item image through CLIP model to get embedding
- Run query (e.g., "navratri day 3 outfit") through CLIP to get embedding
- Cosine similarity produces score 0-1
- **Note:** CLIP alone will not understand "day 3 grey" — this must be composed with color rule logic

**Composition Strategy:**

1. Extract color/day intent from user query
2. Apply Navratri color rule to filter/weight candidates
3. Run CLIP similarity on remaining candidates
4. Combine with weighted formula

### 2.4 Formality Match (25%)

**Formality Tiers:**

| Tier | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| 1 | Casual / Daily Wear | Cotton sarees, casual kurtis, palazzo pants |
| 2 | Semi-Formal / Office | Work-appropriate Indian wear, silk-cotton sarees |
| 3 | Festive / Party | Embroidered sarees, lehengas, anarkalis |
| 4 | Wedding / Bridal | Heavy silk sarees, heavily embellished lehengas |
| 5 | Ceremonial / Sacred | Temple-appropriate simple silk, white/cream sarees |

**Occasion Formality Requirements:**

| Occasion | Expected Tier |
|----------|--------------|
| Diwali (party) | 2-3 |
| Diwali (puja) | 3-4 |
| Navratri | 3 |
| Temple (daily darshan) | 1-2 |
| Temple (special puja) | 3-4 |
| Eid | 2-3 |
| Tamil Wedding (guest) | 3-4 |
| Tamil Wedding (close family) | 4-5 |
| Punjabi Wedding | 3-4 |

**Scoring:**

- Exact tier match = 1.0
- ±1 tier = 0.8
- ±2 tiers = 0.4
- ±3+ tiers = 0.1

### 2.5 Color Appropriateness (15%)

**Mapping per Occasion:**

| Occasion | Auspicious Colors | Forbidden Colors | Neutral |
|----------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| Tamil Wedding | Gold, Deep Red, Maroon, Green, Royal Blue | Black, White | Cream, Pink |
| Punjabi Wedding | Red, Gold, Magenta, Fuchsia, Pastels | Black (minor) | White (modern) |
| Diwali | Gold, Red, Green, Yellow | Black, Dark Blue | Pastels |
| Navratri | Per-day color (see 1.4) | Black (all days) | Off-day: white |
| Temple | Saffron, Yellow, Orange, Red, Cream | Black, White | Green, Blue |
| Eid | White, Cream, Pastels, Emerald | Black (minor), Transparent | Gold accents |

**Scoring:**

- Auspicious color present = 1.0
- Neutral color = 0.5
- Forbidden color = 0.0 (may gate to 0 on Cultural Appropriateness if hard rule)

### 2.6 Heritage Bonus (5%)

**Heritage Registry:**

- User selects primary heritage: Tamil, Punjabi, Bengali, Marathi, Gujarati, Malayali, Telugu, Kannada, Odia, Other
- Items carry heritage tags (learned from CLIP or manual tagging)
- Tamil user + Tamil saree = +0.05 bonus
- Punjabi user + Punjabi lehenga = +0.05 bonus
- Mismatched heritage = no bonus

**Implementation Note:** Heritage is a soft signal; two users of the same heritage may have different preferences. Weight is low to avoid penalizing users who prefer other traditions.

### 2.7 Final Score Calculation

```
final_score = (0.30 × cultural_appropriateness)
            + (0.25 × clip_similarity)
            + (0.25 × formality_match)
            + (0.15 × color_appropriateness)
            + (0.05 × heritage_bonus)
```

**Output:** Ranked list of top-N items (N configurable, default 10).

## 3. Saree Draping Styles

### 3.1 Nivi

**Origin:** Andhra Pradesh / Telangana

**When to Recommend:**

- Formal occasions: weddings (Tamil, Telugu), receptions, engagement ceremonies
- When user wants the most universally recognized elegant drape
- Works for all body types

**Key Characteristics:**

- Pleats folded neatly to the left at the waist
- Pallu (end piece) draped from right shoulder to left hip
- Pallu falls diagonally across the torso
- Blouse covers midriff; no waist exposure

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- Traditional high-neck blouse
- Short sleeves or three-quarter sleeves most common
- Heavily embellished blouses pair well with plain silks

---

### 3.2 Bollywood

**Origin:** Film industry standard; evolved from Nivi with dramatic flair

**When to Recommend:**

- Sangeet, mehendi, cocktail parties
- When user wants glamour with Indian authenticity
- Works well with chiffon, georgette, crepe fabrics

**Key Characteristics:**

- Pallu draped from left shoulder, trailing behind (like a cape)
- Often paired with backless or halter blouses
- More pallu fabric visible; dramatic sweep

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- Typically backless, halter, or off-shoulder blouses
- Heavy work expected on blouse to compensate for simpler drape
- Not appropriate for temples or conservative occasions

---

### 3.3 Maharashtrian

**Origin:** Maharashtra

**When to Recommend:**

- Ganesh Chaturthi, Maharashtra-themed events
- Navratri (acceptable for non-traditional days)
- When user wants comfortable, historical drape

**Key Characteristics:**

- Worn with a non-pleated style; fabric wrapped tightly
- Both ends of saree border visible at bottom (特色)
- No pleats; 6 yards managed differently
- Worn with a fitted bodice ( choli)

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- Fitted, short-sleeve cholis only
- Jewelry: typical Maharashtrian nath (nose ring), paasa
- Color contrasts between saree and choli common

---

### 3.4 Bengali Athpana

**Origin:** Bengal (West Bengal, Bangladesh)

**When to Recommend:**

- Weddings, Saraswati Puja, Durga Puja
- When user wants the distinctive Bengali white-and-red look
- Formal, elegant occasions

**Key Characteristics:**

- White saree (usually Bengali cotton or tant) with red border
- No pleats visible at front; pleats hidden under pallu
- Pallu draped in a specific way called " kodom"
- Heavy gold jewelry emphasis

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- Traditional red or maroon blouses
- Can pair with white blouse if saree is colored
- Jewelry: Bengali gold sets, matha patta (head chain)

---

### 3.5 South Indian Temple Drape

**Origin:** Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Kerala (temple-specific styles)

**When to Recommend:**

- Temple visits, wedding ceremonies (South Indian)
- When user wants maximum traditional authenticity
- Functions requiring modesty (shoulders covered)

**Key Characteristics:**

- Pallu draped to cover shoulders and head
- More fabric coverage than Nivi
- Pleats can be tucked in back or front
- Often worn with underskirt (silk)

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- High neck, full-sleeve blouses
- Traditionally no back exposure
- Temple jewelry: temple necklaces, jhumka earrings

---

### 3.6 Pakistani

**Origin:** Pakistan; popular in Hyderabadi Muslim community

**When to Recommend:**

- Eid (especially Hyderabadi tradition)
- Muslim wedding functions
- When user wants modest coverage

**Key Characteristics:**

- Pallu brought from back to front on the left side
- Often pinned at left shoulder
- More coverage around neck and chest
- Worn with fitted sleeves to wrist

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- High neck; often boat neck or round neck with keyhole
- Full-sleeve blouses standard
- Often paired with white/cream sarees; minimal embellishment

---

### 3.7 Kerala Kasavu

**Origin:** Kerala

**When to Recommend:**

- Onam celebrations
- Temple visits in Kerala
- Formal, sober occasions
- When user wants the iconic Kerala white-and-gold look

**Key Characteristics:**

- White (or cream) saree with gold border (kasavu)
- No pleats; wrapped style
- Traditional Kasavu worn with contrasting-border saree (usually white on white with thick gold zari)
- Worn with a simple tuck; minimal draping

**Blouse Pairing Notes:**

- Simple colored blouses (red, maroon, green)
- No heavy embellishment needed
- Jewelry: Kerala traditional gold (mullappu, nedivilli)

## 4. Dupatta Pairing Rules

### 4.1 Silk + Georgette Pairing

**Principle:** The dupatta fabric should complement or match the main outfit's richness.

| Outfit Fabric | Dupatta Fabric | Notes |
|--------------|----------------|-------|
| Silk (kanjeevaram, banarasi) | Silk dupatta | Heavy silk dupatta matches richness |
| Georgette, chiffon | Same weight georgette/chiffon | Light fabric drapes better |
| Cotton, linen | Cotton with embroidery or light dupatta | Avoid heavy silk with cotton |
| Velvet | Chiffon, net, or light georgette | Prevents overwhelming bulk |
| Crepe | Matching crepe or thin chiffon | Modern, sleek look |

**Anti-pattern:** Pairing a heavily embellished silk dupatta with a simple cotton outfit — the dupatta overwhelms the outfit and signals mismatch.

### 4.2 Color Matching Principles

**Primary Rules:**

1. **Match undertone, not exact shade:** Warm undertone saree + warm undertone dupatta. Cool + cool.
2. **Contrast or complement, never clash:** Red silk saree pairs with gold (complement) or white (contrast). Avoid blue-purple with orange (clash).
3. **Monochrome matching:** White or ivory outfit with contrasting dupatta (e.g., white + gold or white + red).

**Common Successful Pairings:**

| Outfit Color | Dupatta Color |
|-------------|---------------|
| Red silk | Gold, cream, white |
| Blue silk | Silver, white, light blue |
| Green silk | Gold, cream |
| Pink/georgette | Silver, peach, champagne |
| White/cream | Gold, red border dupatta |
| Maroon | Gold, cream, dark pink |

### 4.3 Single vs Double Dupatta

**Single Dupatta (Most Common):**

- Default choice for most occasions
- Worn draped over both shoulders or one shoulder
- Sufficient for: casual occasions, office, moderate festivities

**Double Dupatta:**

- Used for: Bridal wear, heavy wedding functions, ceremonial occasions
- One dupatta draped, second carried or arranged for drama
- Bridal: One covering head, one flowing behind
- Punjabi wedding: Two dupattas carried by bride

**Decision Rule:**

| Occasion | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| Temple visit | Single (modesty) |
| Office | Single |
| Casual festive | Single |
| Sangeet, mehendi | Single or double (outfit-dependent) |
| Wedding ceremony | Double (bride: definitely) |
| Reception | Single |

### 4.4 Lehenga Dupatta vs Scarf Style

**Lehenga Dupatta:**

- Full-length dupatta (usually 2.5-3 meters)
- Draped over one shoulder or carried
- Heavily embellished matching the lehenga
- Worn throughout function; removed only for dancing (Punjabi)

**Scarf Style Dupatta:**

- Shorter dupatta (2 meters or less)
- Loosely draped around neck or carried in hand
- Used for: cocktail parties, fusion events, fashion-forward occasions
- May contrast with lehenga color

**Decision Rule:**

| Lehenga Type | Dupatta Style |
|-------------|-------------|
| Heavy bridal lehenga | Full-length matching dupatta, draped throughout |
| Semi-embroidered party lehenga | Full-length matching or slight contrast |
| Light, pastel lehenga | Scarf style; adds movement without heaviness |
| Contemporary fusion lehenga | Scarf style; fashion-forward |

```sql
CREATE TABLE occasion_profiles (
```

```
### Color Enforcement Priority
For occasions with color rules, weight color appropriateness at 15% but apply
hard gate on forbidden colors. If an item is black at Diwali, it scores 0 on
Cultural Appropriateness, not just color.

### Regional Heritage Routing
If user specifies heritage (Tamil, Punjabi, etc.), apply heritage bonus (+5%)
to items tagged with matching regional origin. Default to no heritage bonus
if user has not specified heritage.

### Saree vs Lehenga Default
When user asks for "wedding outfit" without specifying:
- If user heritage is Tamil, Malayali, Telugu, Kannada → default saree
- If user heritage is Punjabi, Gujarati, Marwari, Rajasthani → default lehenga
- If user heritage is Bengali → default saree (white/red Bengali Athpana)
- Unknown heritage → offer both options

### Dupatta Logic
- Always suggest dupatta pairing if item has dupatta_included: true
- For silk outfits, recommend silk dupatta pairing
- For lehengas, clarify: full dupatta (formal) vs scarf style (party)
- Double dupatta only for: bridal, reception, sangeet
```
